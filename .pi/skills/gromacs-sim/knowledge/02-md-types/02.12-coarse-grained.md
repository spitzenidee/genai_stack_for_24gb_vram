# 02.12 — Coarse-Grained MD (Martini)

**When to use**: simulate **large systems** or **long timescales** (membranes,
lipid rafts, polymers, protein complexes, crowding) by mapping several
atoms per **bead** — the standard is **Martini 3** (4:1 mapping).

## Why CG
- Fewer particles + a **smoothed** potential → larger stable timesteps and
  longer accessible times (µs+), at the cost of atomic detail.
- Martini 3: 4 heavy atoms ≈ 1 bead; a **3-bead** bead model with
  **backmapped** detail; supports **water** (3 water molecules per bead) and
  ions.

## Workflow (differs from all-atom)
1. **Map** the all-atom structure to beads (Martini tooling, e.g. `ac`/
   `martinize`/the Martini mapper). This produces the CG topology + coordinates.
2. **Solvate** with CG water; add CG ions.
3. **Box**: as usual, but CG cutoffs differ from all-atom (Martini uses a
   different cutoff scheme — check the Martini docs; typically ~1.1–1.2 nm
   with a specific `cutoff-scheme`).
4. **Minimize** — CG systems can have **soft-core** issues; use
   **soft-core** equilibration if you get non-finite forces.
5. **Equilibrate** (NVT → NPT) — CG often needs a **longer/softer**
   equilibration; use soft-core during the initial relaxation.
6. **Production** — larger `dt` is possible (Martini ~20–50 fs is common,
   depending on constraints and validation).

## Key differences vs. all-atom

| Aspect | All-atom | Martini CG |
|--------|----------|------------|
| Mapping | 1 atom = 1 particle | 4 atoms ≈ 1 bead |
| Water | explicit molecule | 3 waters/bead |
| Timestep | 2 fs (h-bonds) | ~20–50 fs (validated) |
| Cutoff | ~1.0–1.2 nm | Martini-specific (~1.1–1.2 nm) |
| Soft-core | rare | **common** (equilibration) |
| FF | AMBER/OPLS/CHARMM | Martini 3 |

## Key MDP deltas
- `dt` larger (per Martini validation for your system).
- **Soft-core** options for equilibration (see `02.03` soft-core parameters,
  used here for CG relaxation).
- Martini-specific cutoffs / `cutoff-scheme`.
- Constraints: Martini often uses **no** or different constraints (beads);
  check the Martini recommendations.
- `constraints = none` is common in CG (no SETTLE for CG water).

## Analysis
- CG analysis is at **bead** resolution. Use `gmx` tools on the CG trajectory
  (density, order parameter, distances between beads).
- **Backmapping** (CG → all-atom) is possible for selected frames if atomic
  detail is needed.
- Report results in bead units and note the mapping.

## Pitfalls
- **Non-finite forces** during CG equilibration → use soft-core (see `01.4`).
- Using all-atom cutoffs/water with a CG topology → wrong physics.
- Exceeding the validated timestep → unstable/unphysical dynamics.
- Forgetting to **backmap** when atomic detail is required.
- Martini is a **different ecosystem** (mapper, water, ions, cutoffs) — follow
  the Martini docs, not the all-atom defaults.
